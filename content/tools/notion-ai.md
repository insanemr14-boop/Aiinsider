Notion AI is the clearest example of a category that will define the next few years of software: AI that is worth using not because the model is better, but because it is already looking at your data.

## Context you did not have to assemble

A general assistant knows nothing about your company. To get a useful answer you paste in the project brief, the meeting notes and the spec, and by the time you have done that you could have written the answer.

Notion AI already has them, because they are in Notion. Ask what was decided about a project and it reads the pages. Ask it to draft a status update and it draws on the actual documents rather than on your summary of them. Q&A across the workspace turns an accumulated mess of pages into something searchable by meaning rather than by keyword.

For teams that genuinely run on Notion, that is the entire value proposition, and it is a good one. The permissions model carries through, so the assistant sees what the user sees.

## Where it works

Drafting and rewriting inside a page, where the surrounding document is the context. Summarising long pages and meeting notes. Translating. Extracting action items. The AI-powered database properties — auto-summarising, auto-classifying or extracting a field across every row in a database — are the most underrated feature, because they apply the model to structured work at scale rather than one document at a time.

These are all small wins. They are also the wins people actually collect, because the tool is in the place where the work already happens and requires no decision to use.

## Where it disappoints

Raw output quality trails the dedicated assistants. For serious drafting, [Claude](/tools/claude/) or [ChatGPT](/tools/chatgpt/) produce noticeably better prose, and people who care about writing quality end up drafting elsewhere and pasting in.

Workspace Q&A is only as good as the workspace. Notion instances tend toward entropy — duplicated pages, abandoned databases, three versions of the same policy — and the assistant answers confidently from whichever version it retrieved. The tool surfaces your information architecture problem rather than solving it, and organisations with tidy workspaces get dramatically better results than those without.

Retrieval across a very large workspace is inconsistent, and there is no signal telling you whether an answer was well-grounded or assembled from the wrong pages.

## Pricing

Included in higher Notion plans or sold as a per-seat add-on, which means the decision is usually made for a whole workspace rather than per user.

That makes the pilot question important: measure whether people actually use it, not whether they liked the demo. Embedded AI features have a well-documented gap between licensed seats and weekly active users, and paying per seat for a feature most people never open is an expensive habit.

Cleaning up the workspace before rolling it out will do more for perceived quality than any settings change.

## Who should choose it

Teams already running on Notion who want AI where the work is, and who value convenience over peak output quality. Particularly strong for anyone using Notion databases heavily, where the bulk AI properties do real work.

Teams whose priority is writing quality should keep a dedicated assistant alongside it. Organisations on Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace have the same structural bet available through [Microsoft Copilot](/tools/microsoft-copilot/) or [Gemini](/tools/gemini/), with the same caveats about retrieval and adoption.
